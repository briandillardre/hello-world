# The Path: $100M, then $1B

*Aug 1 2026. Brian: "create a clear path for me to reach $100 million then
$1 billion with HammerTrack." This is that path — valuation milestones, the
math behind each, and the small number of things that must be true at each
gate. Companion docs: GROWTH-PLATFORM.md (the product ladder),
OPERATING-MODEL.md (the cost curve), UNIT-ECONOMICS.md.*

## The one-line thesis

Tracking hardware gets us into the yard; the job data makes us un-rippable;
the financial layer (card, capital, insurance, exit) makes each customer
worth 3–5× their SaaS fee. Software companies are valued at a multiple of
recurring revenue — so the path to $1B is arithmetic:
**~$10–12M ARR ≈ $100M valuation · ~$90–120M ARR ≈ $1B** (8–10× forward
ARR for growing vertical SaaS; fintech attach pushes the multiple up, slow
growth pushes it down).

## The market is big enough — that's not the risk

~750,000 US construction firms with payroll; ~3.5M including
owner-operators; service/field companies (landscaping, septic, utilities,
ag) roughly double it. $1B needs ~20,000 customers at ~$5k/yr blended
(SaaS + fintech take) — **under 3% of just the US construction segment.**
The risk is never TAM; it's distribution and retention.

- The only metrics that matter: logo retention (25/25 stay) and weekly
  active owners (do they open the map when nothing is stolen?).
- Funding: none. $130/mo burn, Mercury account, Brian's time.
- **Gate to pass: 25 companies, 3+ referrals that came unprompted.**

## Stage 1 → $1M ARR (2027–2028) · proves distribution repeats
**~350 companies · $250/mo blended · valuation ~$6–10M.**
- Channel #1: the theft-hook ad funnel (already built) run properly in
  Upstate SC first, then the Charlotte and Atlanta corridors (Nashville was
  demo fiction — you sell where people already return your calls).
- Channel #2 (the unlock): **equipment dealers as resellers** — the dealer
  installs a tracker with every used-iron sale, splits first-year revenue.
  One productive dealer = 5–15 customers/yr forever. Sign 10.
- Expansion revenue starts: PM tiers ($49/$199) raise blended ACV without
  new logos.
- Team: Brian + S1 (part-time field sales & install, Sep 2026 — hired for
  the rolodex, absorbs the installer role) → H2 (full-time ops/install tech)
  at ~90 customers, per docs/OPERATING-MODEL.md. Funding: still optional —
  revenue + maybe $250–500k angel/friends round if dealer channel needs
  install subsidy.
- **Gate: CAC < 6 months of gross profit; net revenue retention > 100%.**

## Stage 2 → $10–12M ARR ≈ **$100M** (2029–2030) · proves the platform
**~2,500 companies · $400/mo blended · 3 revenue engines running.**
1. **SaaS** (~60% of revenue): tracking + PM + field ops across the
   Southeast → national self-serve.
2. **Fintech attach** (~30%): HammerTrack Card interchange + equipment-
   finance referral fees + insurance referrals (GROWTH-PLATFORM layers 2/3/5a
   — all gated items unlock in this stage).
3. **Data products** (~10%): benchmarks, valuation/exit-readiness upsell.
- The compounding trick: fintech revenue grows with customer SPEND, not
  seat count — a flat logo month can still be a +8% revenue month.
- Team ~30–40 (sales pods by metro, 3–4 engineers, fintech ops, support).
- Funding decision point: a $8–15M Series A here buys speed (dealer network
  + card program capital); the bootstrap alternative caps growth ~40%/yr
  but keeps Brian >80%. **$100M valuation × 80% beats $100M × 35% — raise
  only if the card program or a competitor forces the pace.**
- **Gate: NRR ≥ 115%, gross margin ≥ 75%, fintech attach ≥ 25% of new
  cohort revenue.**

## Stage 3 → $90–120M ARR ≈ **$1B** (2031–2034) · proves the category
**~20,000 customers · $450–550/mo blended · "the operating system for
companies that own iron."**
- **Verticals beyond construction** (same product, new ad hooks): landscaping,
  septic/excavation services, utility contractors, ag custom operators,
  municipal fleets. Each is a construction-shaped company with trucks,
  machines, crews, and jobs.
- **Fintech at maturity**: card spend at scale (interchange on $1B+/yr of
  customer spend ≈ $20–25M alone), working-capital advances via a capital
  partner, embedded insurance agency renewing commissions annually.
- **The exit suite becomes a marketplace**: companies sell THROUGH
  HammerTrack (we hold the diligence data) — success fees on $5–50M
  business sales; every seller hands us the buyer as a customer.
- **Geography**: Canada + Australia (same iron, same English, same theft
  problem) — not Europe.
- Team 250–350. This stage almost certainly takes institutional capital
  (Series B/C) or a strategic partner (an OEM, a Ramp, an insurer) — decide
  then, from strength.
- **Gate: this is where you sell, IPO, or keep it — at ≥$90M ARR growing
  30%+, all three are live options. The plan's job is to make it a choice.**

## What kills it (watch these, nothing else)
1. **Churn** — everything compounds off retention; a 2%/mo churn business
   never reaches Stage 2. The self-feeding data + weekly digests + valuation
   card exist precisely to make leaving feel like amputation.
2. **Hardware distraction** — hardware stays pass-through at cost forever.
   The moment we carry inventory margin, we're Tenna with worse funding.
3. **Fintech shortcuts** — partner-first, never balance-sheet risk, never
   unlicensed. One compliance fire in Stage 2 erases the decade.
4. **Enterprise seduction** — a $200k regional-fleet deal in Stage 1 would
   consume the year that was supposed to produce 300 small GCs. Say no
   until Stage 3.
5. **Founder bus-factor** — by Stage 2 the AI advisor + playbooked ops must
   run without Brian in every loop (this is also what the exit multiple
   prices).
