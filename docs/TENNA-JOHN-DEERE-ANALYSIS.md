# Tenna — Deep-Dive Analysis & John Deere Acquisition

**Prepared:** July 9, 2026 · For HammerTrack business/competitive planning
**Why this matters:** Tenna is HammerTrack's headline competitor and its closest exit comp. In Dec 2025 it was acquired by John Deere — the clearest signal yet that this category is real and that strategics will pay for it.

---

## ⚠️ Two corrections to the common story

1. **The "$400 million" price is unconfirmed.** Deere & Company and every major trade outlet (ENR, Equipment World, ForConstructionPros, Yahoo Finance, World Construction Network) call the terms **undisclosed**. No primary source — including Deere's own press release and SEC filing searches — states a number. Treat $400M as an unverified rumor/estimate, not fact.
2. **Tenna is not related to Rhino Fleet Tracking.** Tenna was founded as **BuildSourced** and is a portfolio holding of **The Conti Group** (a 113-year-old infrastructure developer in New Hope, PA). Rhino Fleet Tracking is a separate Texas GPS company — no connection.

## Timeline

- **Jan 2015 — Founded as BuildSourced.** Austin Conti and Jose Cueva start the company to unify IoT tracking (GPS/BLE/RFID) into one view of a construction fleet. The two met on a ~$67M NCDOT infrastructure project (Conti superintendent, Cueva project engineer). Conti's family firm, **The Conti Group**, becomes the backer.
- **Sep 27, 2017 — Rebrand to Tenna.** New logo, expanded product line, tagline **"Find More."**
- **Q1 2018 — Hardware line ships.** RFID trackers, Bluetooth asset tags, cellular/GPS transmitters, LoRa long-range trackers — a full self-branded hardware stack, not pure software.
- **2020–2024 — Hypergrowth.** Cited 2,200%+ three-year growth (2020–2023); ~739–740% (2021–2024). Inc. 5000 four straight years, peaking at **No. 162 (2024)**, then **No. 552 (2025)**.
- **Nov 2024 — ~$15M revenue, ~124 employees** (Latka; self-reported).
- **Dec 22, 2025 — Deere announces agreement** to acquire Tenna from The Conti Group. Terms undisclosed.
- **~Feb 18, 2026 — Deal closes.** Tenna operates as an **independent business under the Tenna brand**, keeping its mixed-fleet, multi-brand model.

---

## The John Deere deal

- **Buyer:** Deere & Company. **Seller:** The Conti Group. **Target:** 100% of Tenna.
- **Structure:** Tenna stays independent, keeps its name, keeps selling to any contractor regardless of equipment brand.
- **Strategic rationale (Deere's framing):** move beyond "iron-only" digital tools. Contractors increasingly run **mixed fleets** (Cat, Komatsu, Volvo, Deere) and want one data layer across all of it. Tenna lets Deere position as a **jobsite-ecosystem technology provider**, not just an OEM with telematics locked to its own machines. It's a data/software play: utilization, maintenance, uptime, cross-brand cost analytics.

---

## Product & pricing

- **Platform:** unifies equipment, vehicles, attachments, tools, and materials in one system — real-time location, utilization, preventive maintenance, safety/compliance, inspections, and increasingly **AI** (dash cams, driver scorecards, custom inspections).
- **Hardware:** its own GPS, BLE, RFID, and LoRa trackers, sold with the software.
- **Pricing:** custom quote only (products × asset types × asset quantity), monthly billing, no long-term contracts. Third-party estimates put core tracking around **$3–8/asset/month** — an estimate, not a published rate card.

> **Correction for our own docs:** Tenna is *not* simply "$15–25/asset + $500 setup, no Bluetooth tools." It *does* offer BLE tool tags, and its street pricing is closer to ~$3–8/asset. Our differentiation must lean on **QuickBooks-native billing/job-costing, 2 AM theft-SMS, tool tracking included at a low flat rate, local hands-on service, and SMB focus** — not price alone.

## What this means for HammerTrack

1. **The category is validated and the exit comp is now public.** A construction-operator-founded, mixed-fleet tracker (Tenna) scaled to ~$15M revenue and sold to a top-tier strategic. That is *exactly* HammerTrack's playbook and thesis, proven by a buyer writing a check.
2. **Deere ownership pulls Tenna upmarket.** Deere's incentive is large mixed-fleet contractors and dealer channels — not the 5-truck Upstate GC. That **leaves the SMB/owner-operator beachhead more open**, which is precisely where HammerTrack is aimed.
3. **Deere is now a plausible future acquirer of HammerTrack** — or a competitor whose Tenna push makes a rival strategic (Caterpillar, Komatsu, EquipmentShare, Samsara) want an SMB-focused answer. Either way, more strategic appetite in the space is good for our exit.
4. **Update the pitch, not the price.** With Tenna's real pricing closer to ours, "half the price" is a weaker wedge than we assumed. Lead with QBO-native + theft-SMS + tools-included + local service + SMB simplicity.
