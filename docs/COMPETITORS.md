## 2. Competitor by Competitor

### Tenna — the named rival

**What they are.** Construction-specific asset management: mixed-fleet GPS, maintenance/work orders, utilization, dash cams, ERP integrations (Viewpoint Vista, Procore). Acquired by **John Deere** (closed ~Feb 2026); operates independently under the Tenna name, but is now OEM-owned.

**Pricing reality.** No published pricing — quote, negotiate, sign. Third-party estimates: ~$15–30/asset/mo software, $100–500/device hardware, and **implementation fees pegged at $5,000–$20,000 for SMBs** (ITQlick). A verified Capterra reviewer: "total implementation costs were very high and not explained fully" and "charged for every extra report."

**Where they beat us.** Enterprise depth: Viewpoint Vista work-order integration, Procore daily logs, WEX fuel cards, AI dash cams, mature dispatch/scheduling, onsite training, hundreds of references — and now Deere's balance sheet and dealer channel. At a 200-asset heavy-civil contractor running Vista, they win.

**Where we beat them.** Price and friction — about half their cost with $0 setup versus a negotiated contract plus a five-figure implementation. The segment they openly don't serve: small crews. QuickBooks-native job costing (theirs is claimed but shallow). Reliability narrative: their most persistent review complaint is trackers going offline — one admin spent 5 months chasing dead devices. And neutrality: post-Deere, mixed-fleet contractors (Cat, Komatsu, Case shops) have a fresh reason to ask who's reading their fleet data.

**Important honesty note:** Tenna DOES have BLE small-tool tracking now (TennaINTEL + TennaBLE RT1 beacons) and claims QuickBooks integration. Don't say otherwise — sell on implementation cost, contract friction, small-crew fit, and reliability instead.

**Counter-line:** *"Tenna's great — if you've got twenty grand for the implementation and a year to negotiate the contract. Ask them what setup actually costs before you sign."*

### Samsara — the trucking giant coming downmarket

**What they are.** Public IoT company (~$1.9B ARR) built on trucking telematics: ELD, AI dashcams, driver safety. Construction has been their fastest-growing vertical for 10 straight quarters, and their Bluetooth Asset Tags (located via every Samsara gateway on the road) tripled ARR YoY.

**Pricing reality.** Quote-only. Reported: $27–33/vehicle/mo base, $40–60 with cameras and analytics. **3-year minimum contract**; fleets of 10 or fewer must prepay the full 3 years plus hardware upfront. Full remaining balance due on early exit. Multiple 2025–26 reviews report billing starts at hardware ship date, not install date.

**Where they beat us.** AI dashcams and driver coaching (we have speed-based grades, no video). ELD compliance if the prospect runs DOT-regulated trucks. Hardware polish at scale. The crowd-locate network for stolen assets that leave the county. Brand trust with insurers and procurement. At a 100+ vehicle GC with a safety department, they win.

**Where we beat them.** Price and terms — no 3-year prepaid handcuffs. The entire money loop they don't have: QuickBooks job-cost invoices, per-zone budget burn, receipt capture, punch lists. Tool tags that work through your own trucks rather than depending on Samsara device density — which is thin on rural Southeast job sites. OEM telematics ingestion so a newer machine tracks with no extra hardware. And their #1 review complaint is customer support ("Poor Customer Support" appears ~260 times as a con on G2).

**Counter-line:** *"Ask them two things: is the whole three years due upfront, and does it push job costs into QuickBooks? Then call me back."*

### Verizon Connect — the contract trap

**What they are.** Verizon's fleet telematics rollup (Fleetmatics + Telogis). Reveal GPS tracking, dashcams, ELD, plus an equipment asset tracker line. A generalist fleet platform sold to fleet managers, with construction as one vertical page.

**Pricing reality.** Quote-only. Reported entry ~$23.50/vehicle/mo on a **36-month auto-renewing contract**; realistic range $20–55 with video. You never own the hardware — it's returned at contract end. Documented fees: $150/device professional install, another $150/device to move or replace one. Cancellation requires notice inside a 60–90 day window or you're renewed; BBB shows a failing grade with 100+ unresolved complaints, including a reported $30k+ demanded after timely notice.

**Where they beat us.** ELD/HOS + IFTA compliance, AI dashcams, a nationwide professional install network, carrier-grade coverage story, enterprise reporting depth.

**Where we beat them.** Nearly everything a GC actually needs: BLE tool tracking (they have zero small-tool answer), QuickBooks job costing (theirs requires a paid API tier plus a third party), zones with hours/cost ledgers, OEM telematics ingestion, after-hours theft alerts as a first-class feature. Their Capterra support score is 2.8/5 across 940 reviews. And the structural one: they own your hardware and three years of your budget.

**Counter-line:** *"They own your hardware and three years of your budget — and it's $150 every time you want to move a tracker to another machine. We charge monthly and you own everything."*

### FleetWatcher (AlignOps) — the paving specialist

**What they are.** ~20-year-old heavy-civil materials management platform: plant-to-paver load cycles, DOT-accepted e-ticketing, third-party hauler pay, equipment utilization. Acquired by AlignOps (Aug 2024), now bundled with ToolWatch, busybusy, and Safety Reports. Their famous ROI story (cutting ~5 excess haul trucks at ~$1k/truck/day) is real — for paving logistics operations.

**Pricing reality.** Quote-only. Third-party estimates: $20–60/vehicle/mo plus implementation of $500–$5,000 for small/mid fleets and $15,000+ for complex ones. Proprietary hardware installed per machine. Contract terms not disclosed (unverified).

**Where they beat us.** Anything touching material hauling: plant-scale integration, load-cycle analysis, DOT e-ticketing, virtual shift tickets, truck pay. Enterprise heavy-civil credibility (Colas, CRH) and Viewpoint/B2W integrations. If the RFP says "e-ticketing" or "DOT project," don't fight it.

**Where we beat them.** Everywhere the buyer is a GC or sitework contractor rather than a paving logistics operation. Their BLE tool tracking is a manual 30-second phone scan with a cooldown, not passive inheritance. No QuickBooks integration found anywhere. No theft-alert story. Reviews call their post-rollout navigation "muddier." A 10–30 asset contractor without a plant scale pays enterprise prices for GPS + reports.

**Counter-line:** *"FleetWatcher is a paving logistics system. If you're not hauling asphalt against a plant scale, you're paying for a materials platform to do what a map does."*

### GPS Trackit — the budget vehicle tracker

**What they are.** 20+ year value-priced GPS fleet tracking (Roswell, GA; PE-backed). Vehicle tracking, geofences, dashcam and ELD add-ons, one solar asset tracker SKU. Acquired Zonar (Dec 2024) and is pushing toward trucking and school-bus fleets.

**Pricing reality.** Quote-only despite the value positioning; reported ~$23.95–35.95/vehicle/mo by tier. Hardware is "free" — meaning leased, returned on cancellation or you're charged. Month-to-month headline, but their own policy page pairs a 30-day guarantee with a 90-day cancellation-notice policy, and Trustpilot/BBB complaints report billing continuing months after cancellation.

**Where they beat us.** Dashcams with event video, certified ELD, no upfront hardware cost, a long-tenured brand with a national install org.

**Where we beat them.** Their model collapses on a mixed construction fleet — a 10-truck / 15-machine / 50-tool contractor is unpriceable on per-vehicle SKUs. No BLE tools, no job-site cost ledger, no QuickBooks job costing found, no OEM ingestion. Reviewers complain core features are paid add-ons. Customer owns nothing.

**Counter-line:** *"That's a truck tracker. What's their answer for your excavator, your skid steer, and the $40k of tools in the trailer? Ours is one map."*

### EquipmentShare T3 — the rental company's operating system

**What they are.** The telematics platform of EquipmentShare, the rental giant that IPO'd January 2026 (~$4.65B TTM revenue, ~77% from rentals). T3 comes free on every machine they rent out and is sold standalone to contractors: any-OEM telematics, keypad access control, dash cams, time cards, work orders.

**Pricing reality.** No published pricing anywhere. Third-party estimates ~$30–70/asset/mo (unverified). Per their published terms: 12-month initial term, **auto-renews in 12-month blocks unless you give notice 90+ days before expiration**. Their ToS also says EquipmentShare owns "Service Data" derived from your usage.

**Where they beat us.** Hardware breadth (cams, keypads, ELD, CAN health data), predictive maintenance trained on a nationwide rental fleet, a deeper workforce layer, the Milwaukee One-Key tool partnership, and unbeatable distribution — anyone renting from their branches gets T3 seeded free.

**Where we beat them.** Independence. Their software exists partly to show them which machines you under-utilize — feeding the rent-don't-own pitch. Published pricing vs. a sales call. Month-to-month vs. a 90-day-notice auto-renewal. Tool tags on any brand, not just Milwaukee. QuickBooks job costing. And self-serve speed for the 5–50 asset fleet their enterprise motion ignores. Notably, T3 has almost no independent review footprint — quality is unverifiable outside vendor testimonials.

**Counter-line:** *"T3 is free until you notice who's reading your utilization data — and what renewal notice you owe them 90 days in advance."*

### Trackunit — the OEM machine-health backbone

**What they are.** Danish equipment telematics (founded 2003, majority-owned by Goldman Sachs Alternatives since 2025). 6.5M+ connected assets; the white-label backbone for major OEMs and mega-rentals like Sunbelt. Best-in-class CAN machine health, operator access control, and Bluetooth Kin tags for tools.

**Pricing reality.** Quote-only, no numbers published anywhere. **36–42 month contracts typical** per multiple third-party sources. Hardware sold separately, wired installs required for the good data.

**Where they beat us.** Machine-health depth — factory-grade fault codes, true hours, fuel burn, predictive maintenance. Operator access control (PIN/keycard machine lockout) — we have nothing there. If a customer's dealer or rental house already runs Trackunit, it's pre-wired.

**Where we beat them.** They don't track trucks — a mixed truck+equipment contractor needs a second system; we're one map. Zero money loop: no QuickBooks, no job costing, no receipts, no time clock. Their reviews describe unreachable support ("the only way to speak to someone is to press the option for buying a new contract"), cancellation fights, trackers draining machine batteries, and stale data. And here's the judo move: **their AEMP/ISO 15143-3 feeds are our friend** — HammerTrack's OEM ingestion can pull Trackunit-connected machines into our map, so a customer keeps factory telematics and still standardizes on us.

**Counter-line:** *"Trackunit is what your dealer runs. Keep it — we'll pull that data in over the standard feed, and put your trucks, tools, and job costs on the same map, which they'll never do."*

### HCSS Telematics — the heavy-civil suite attach

**What they are.** The GPS module of the dominant heavy-civil software suite (HeavyBid, HeavyJob, Equipment360). Owned by **Nemetschek since July 2026**. Telematics exists to feed GPS-verified machine hours into HeavyJob job costing — it's an attach product, not a standalone tracker. Their OEM Link pulls 30+ factory telematics feeds.

**Pricing reality.** Quote-only. Third-party trackers cite ~$11–20/asset/mo plus upfront hardware (unverified). The suite context is expensive — HeavyBid alone starts around $4,000/yr for one user — and reviewers plan about a month of training.

**Where they beat us.** If the prospect already runs HeavyBid/HeavyJob, GPS hours reconciling automatically into their existing job-cost loop is unbeatable. Walk away from that bake-off. Also: 30+ OEM feeds, satellite tracking for no-cell sites, DOT e-ticketing, 4,000-client trust.

**Where we beat them.** They structurally can't serve our buyer: a QuickBooks-based GC gets no job-cost story from HCSS at all. Their base heavy-equipment plan pings as slowly as **every 4 hours** — not a live map. No small-tool tracking. Dated interface, documented mobile complaints, month-long learning curve, quote-only with upfront hardware.

**Counter-line:** *"HCSS is the DOT-contractor suite — if you run HeavyJob, use it. If your books are in QuickBooks, their telematics has nothing to plug into. Ours does."*

**Pricing reality.** Published (credit where due): ~$22.99–25/vehicle/mo, asset trackers ~$15/mo, dashcam service ~$25/mo. The hook: hardware is $69.99 with no contract but **free with a 3-year contract** — and Trustpilot/complaint sites document refusal to cancel, silent auto-renewals, and billing after cancellation.

**Where they beat us.** Buy-it-this-afternoon friction — no sales conversation at all. A shipping solar tracker for non-powered assets. Years of SEO and Amazon presence.

**Where we beat them.** 60-second refresh vs. our seconds-level stream when moving. Their tool trackers are last-seen-location, not live inheritance through your trucks. No zones-as-job-sites, no cost ledger, no work orders, no OEM ingestion, no QuickBooks job costing (mileage sync only). The "free hardware" is a 3-year handcuff.

**Counter-line:** *"Linxup tells you where a dot is. It can't tell you what the job cost, who had the drill, or that your skid steer moved at 2 AM. And that free tracker costs three years."*

**Where they beat us.** AI dashcams far beyond our speed-stream grades, ELD/DOT compliance (disqualifying for us if the prospect runs CDL trucks over the line), fuel cards, IPO-scale sales muscle.

**Where we beat them.** Construction is their marketing vertical, not their product core: no small-tool tracking, no receipt capture, no per-zone cost ledger; QuickBooks is accounting sync, not job costing. Overkill and overpriced for a 10-truck GC with no ELD requirement. Their own review pages are our ad copy.

**Counter-line:** *"Motive is a trucking compliance platform with a construction brochure. If you don't need ELD, you're paying camera-fleet prices for a map — check their BBB page before you sign the three-year deal."*

## 3. How We Win — The Durable Wedges

Honest scorecard. A wedge only counts if the field genuinely leaves it open.

**Wedge 1: Published price, $0 setup, no contract handcuffs.**
Eight of ten competitors are quote-only. Six run multi-year contracts, several with auto-renewal traps that generate BBB complaints we can read aloud. Only Linxup publishes prices — and their "free hardware" is a 3-year lock. Nobody in this field offers transparent pricing, $0 setup, month-to-month, and customer-owned hardware together. **Who could close it:** Samsara or Motive could publish an SMB SKU tomorrow; nothing stops them but their own sales-org economics. This wedge is structural for the enterprise players and cultural for the rest — durable for 2–3 years, not forever.

**Wedge 2: The money loop — GPS to QuickBooks job cost.**
Trucks, machines, and tools feeding hours and costs into zones, and zones feeding QuickBooks job-cost invoices, receipts, and budgets. Nobody here does this. Tenna and HCSS integrate with ERPs our buyer doesn't run (Vista, HeavyJob). Samsara/Motive/Linxup do accounting sync at best. Trackunit and T3 have no accounting story for small GCs at all. **Who could close it:** HCSS if they ever built a QBO bridge (against their DNA and their new owner's upmarket pull); Tenna if they deepened their claimed QBO support. Watch Tenna here. This is our deepest moat — defend it by going deeper (estimates, change orders, pay apps), not wider.

**Wedge 3: The whole fleet of a small contractor on one map — including the $200 drill.**
Trucks (OBD2) + machines (GPS or free OEM feed) + tools (passive BLE inheriting truck location) + people. Trackunit doesn't do trucks. GPS Trackit/Linxup/Motive/Verizon don't really do tools. FleetWatcher's tool answer is a manual phone scan. T3's is Milwaukee-only. **Who closes it:** Tenna genuinely does (TennaINTEL + RT1), and Samsara's Asset Tags get better as their network densifies. Here we win on price-per-covered-asset and on working without anyone else's network density — but not on uniqueness. Sell it as "all of it, at a price that works for 20 assets," not "only we can."

**Wedge 4: Built for the owner, not the fleet manager.**
Every competitor sells to someone whose job is fleets. Our buyer runs jobs. Theft alerts on his phone at 2 AM, a morning briefing before the crews roll, receipt chase the second a card swipes, a map that answers "where's my money and my day." No one in this field is even trying to build that product for a 10–50 asset contractor. **Who could close it:** an EquipmentShare or Samsara SMB skunkworks. The defense is speed — keep shipping owner-shaped features faster than an enterprise vendor can approve a roadmap slide.

## 4. Threats Worth Watching

| Threat | Worry when… |
|---|---|
| **Samsara packages a construction SMB SKU.** Construction is their fastest-growing vertical; Asset Tags tripled ARR. | They publish pricing, drop the 3-year prepay for small fleets, or ship a construction-specific bundle under ~$15/asset. |
| **Tenna under Deere goes downmarket or gets cheap.** Deere's dealer channel touches every contractor we sell to. | Tenna announces self-serve signup, published pricing, or a Deere-dealer bundle; or shows up in a sub-50-asset deal we're in. |
| **EquipmentShare seeds T3 free across the Southeast.** Every rental transaction is a distribution event, and they have branches in our beachhead. | Prospects start saying "we already have T3 from our rentals" in discovery calls; or T3 ships a real QuickBooks integration. |
| **AlignOps bundles tools + time + fleet.** They own ToolWatch and busybusy — the pieces of an SMB suite exist under one roof. | AlignOps launches a unified small-contractor bundle with published pricing rather than four separate enterprise products. |
| **Trackunit's Kin tags + OEM factory-fit reach small fleets.** Factory-installed telematics erodes the "add a tracker" market from above. | OEMs start including Trackunit-class connectivity free on compact equipment, or Trackunit ships a self-serve contractor tier. |
| **Motive's IPO war chest points at construction.** ~100k customers and a sales machine, one vertical pivot away. | Post-IPO, Motive ships small-tool tracking or job-costing features instead of just construction marketing pages. |
| **A price-transparency copycat.** Our sharpest wedge is also the easiest to imitate on a pricing page. | Any credible entrant publishes per-asset pricing with $0 setup aimed at small GCs — then the fight moves to the money loop, where we want it. |

**Standing rule for the sales floor:** never bluff on a competitor's gaps. Tenna has tool tags. Samsara's cameras are excellent. HCSS owns heavy civil. We win on price transparency, the QuickBooks money loop, whole-fleet coverage at small-contractor scale, and owner-first speed — say that, prove it in a demo, and let their contract terms and review pages do the rest.
